Where deals stall

Carbon buyers do not buy a folder of PDFs.

They need a coherent chain from project claim to current evidence, quantity, authority and status. Small contradictions create big pauses.

  • 01
    Claims do not match documents

    Public copy, pitch materials and diligence files use different quantities, vintages, methodologies or status language.

  • 02
    Authority is assumed, not shown

    The right to market, represent or transfer the inventory is unclear, incomplete or spread across several parties.

  • 03
    Inventory is not reconciled

    Issued, retired, reserved, sold and available units are not tied to one current source of truth.

  • 04
    The buyer brief overreaches

    Marketing language gets ahead of validation, verification, issuance or other documented milestones.

Decision language

A useful answer, even when the answer is not yet.

GO

Ready for a qualified conversation

The supplied evidence supports a controlled buyer discussion, subject to the limits and open items recorded.

HOLD

Promising, but close the gaps first

One or more material inconsistencies or missing documents should be resolved before broader marketing.

NO-GO

Do not market this inventory yet

The supplied record does not currently support the proposed buyer-facing claim, authority or availability position.

These are internal commercial-readiness findings, not an assurance opinion, certification decision or legal conclusion.

Intake

Bring the current record, including the awkward parts.

The sprint works best when the source material is complete enough to compare claims, quantities, roles and status.

  • Project summary, geography, activity and named parties
  • Methodology, standard and current validation or verification status
  • Registry links, project IDs, serial ranges or issuance records, if applicable
  • Documents supplied for title, marketing authority and transfer pathway
  • Vintage, issued, retired, sold, reserved and available quantity records
  • Current website, deck, term sheet, buyer brief and other public claims
  • Known disputes, reversals, changes, restrictions or unresolved diligence questions
